## Lesson 1: Three-Dimensional Figures

### Types of 3D Figures

**Prisms:** Two parallel, congruent bases connected by rectangular faces
- Rectangular prism
- Triangular prism
- Hexagonal prism

**Pyramids:** One base, triangular faces meet at a point (apex)
- Rectangular pyramid
- Triangular pyramid
- Square pyramid

**Cylinders:** Two circular bases connected by a curved surface

**Cones:** One circular base, curved surface meets at a point (apex)

**Spheres:** All points equidistant from center

### Parts of 3D Figures

**Face:** A flat surface
**Edge:** Where two faces meet
**Vertex:** Where edges meet (a corner)
**Base:** The bottom (and sometimes top) face
**Apex:** The top point of a pyramid or cone

## Lesson 2: Volume of Prisms

### Volume Formula for Prisms

**Volume = Base Area × Height**

**V = Bh** where B is the area of the base

### Rectangular Prism

**Volume = length × width × height**
**V = lwh**

**Example 1:** Find the volume
```
Length = 6 cm, Width = 4 cm, Height = 5 cm
V = 6 × 4 × 5
V = 120 cm³
```

### Triangular Prism

**Base is a triangle:** B = ½bh
**Volume:** V = (½bh) × height of prism

**Example:** Triangular base with base 6 cm and height 4 cm, prism height 10 cm
```
Base area: B = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm²
Volume: V = 12 × 10 = 120 cm³
```

---

## Lesson 3: Volume of Cylinders

### Cylinder Volume Formula

**Volume = π × radius² × height**
**V = πr²h**

**Example 1:** Find the volume (r = 3 cm, h = 10 cm)
```
V = π × 3² × 10
V = π × 9 × 10
V = 90π cm³
V ≈ 282.6 cm³ (using π ≈ 3.14)
```

**Example 2:** Find the volume (diameter = 8 in, h = 5 in)
```
Radius = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 inches
V = π × 4² × 5
V = π × 16 × 5
V = 80π in³
V ≈ 251.2 in³
```

---

## Lesson 4: Volume of Pyramids and Cones

### Pyramid Volume Formula

**Volume = ⅓ × Base Area × Height**
**V = ⅓Bh**

**Example:** Square pyramid with base 6 cm and height 9 cm
```
Base area: B = 6² = 36 cm²
V = ⅓ × 36 × 9
V = ⅓ × 324
V = 108 cm³
```

### Cone Volume Formula

**Volume = ⅓ × π × radius² × height**
**V = ⅓πr²h**

**Example:** Cone with radius 4 cm and height 6 cm
```
V = ⅓ × π × 4² × 6
V = ⅓ × π × 16 × 6
V = ⅓ × 96π
V = 32π cm³
V ≈ 100.48 cm³
```

**Key concept:** Pyramids and cones have ⅓ the volume of prisms and cylinders with the same base and height!

## Lesson 5: Volume of Spheres

### Sphere Volume Formula

**Volume = 4/3 × π × radius³**
**V = 4/3πr³**

**Example 1:** Find the volume (r = 3 cm)
```
V = 4/3 × π × 3³
V = 4/3 × π × 27
V = 36π cm³
V ≈ 113.04 cm³
```

**Example 2:** Find the volume (diameter = 10 inches)
```
Radius = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 inches
V = 4/3 × π × 5³
V = 4/3 × π × 125
V = 500π/3 in³
V ≈ 523.33 in³
```

## Lesson 6: Surface Area of Prisms and Cylinders

### Rectangular Prism Surface Area

**Surface Area = 2(lw + lh + wh)**

**Example:** l = 5 cm, w = 3 cm, h = 4 cm
```
SA = 2(5×3 + 5×4 + 3×4)
SA = 2(15 + 20 + 12)
SA = 2(47)
SA = 94 cm²
```

### Cylinder Surface Area

**Surface Area = 2πr² + 2πrh**
(Two circular bases + curved surface)

**Example:** r = 4 cm, h = 10 cm
```
SA = 2π(4)² + 2π(4)(10)
SA = 2π(16) + 2π(40)
SA = 32π + 80π
SA = 112π cm²
SA ≈ 351.68 cm²
```

## Lesson 7: Cross-Sections of 3D Figures

### What is a Cross-Section?

A **cross-section** is the 2D shape you see when you slice through a 3D figure.

### Common Cross-Sections

**Rectangular Prism:**
- Horizontal slice → Rectangle
- Vertical slice → Rectangle or square

**Cylinder:**
- Horizontal slice → Circle
- Vertical slice → Rectangle

**Cone:**
- Horizontal slice → Circle
- Vertical slice through apex → Triangle

**Sphere:**
- Any slice → Circle

**Pyramid:**
- Horizontal slice → Shape of base (smaller)
- Vertical slice through apex → Triangle

## Lesson 8: Angle Relationships

### Types of Angle Pairs

**Complementary Angles:** Two angles that sum to 90°
```
Example: 30° and 60° are complementary
```

**Supplementary Angles:** Two angles that sum to 180°
```
Example: 110° and 70° are supplementary
```

**Vertical Angles:** Opposite angles formed by intersecting lines (always equal)
```
When two lines cross:
   ∠1 and ∠3 are vertical
   ∠2 and ∠4 are vertical
   Vertical angles are congruent
```

**Adjacent Angles:** Angles that share a vertex and a side

**Linear Pair:** Adjacent angles that form a straight line (sum to 180°)

## Lesson 9: Angles in Triangles

### Triangle Angle Sum

**The sum of angles in ANY triangle is 180°**

**Example 1:** Find the missing angle
```
Two angles are 50° and 70°
Third angle: 180° - 50° - 70° = 60°
```

**Example 2:** Find x
```
Angles are x, 2x, and 3x
x + 2x + 3x = 180
6x = 180
x = 30°
Angles are: 30°, 60°, 90°
```

### Exterior Angles

**Exterior Angle Theorem:** An exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles

**Example:**
```
Interior angles: 50° and 70°
Exterior angle: 50° + 70° = 120°
```

## Lesson 10: Types of Triangles

### By Angles

**Acute Triangle:** All angles less than 90°

**Right Triangle:** One angle equals 90°

**Obtuse Triangle:** One angle greater than 90°

**Equiangular Triangle:** All angles equal 60°

### By Sides

**Scalene Triangle:** No sides equal

**Isosceles Triangle:** Two sides equal (two angles equal)

**Equilateral Triangle:** All sides equal (all angles 60°)

### Special Properties

**Isosceles Triangle:** Base angles are equal
```
If two sides are equal, the angles opposite them are equal
```

**Equilateral Triangle:**
```
All sides equal
All angles equal 60°
Is also isosceles and acute
```

## Lesson 11: The Pythagorean Theorem

### The Formula

**In a right triangle:** a² + b² = c²

Where:
- a and b are the legs (sides forming the right angle)
- c is the hypotenuse (side opposite the right angle, longest side)

**Example 1:** Find the hypotenuse
```
Legs: a = 3, b = 4
3² + 4² = c²
9 + 16 = c²
25 = c²
c = 5
```

**Example 2:** Find the missing leg
```
Leg a = 6, hypotenuse c = 10
6² + b² = 10²
36 + b² = 100
b² = 64
b = 8
```

**Example 3:** Is this a right triangle?
```
Sides: 5, 12, 13
Check: 5² + 12² = 13²
25 + 144 = 169
169 = 169 ✓
Yes, it's a right triangle!
```

## Lesson 12: Special Right Triangles

### Pythagorean Triples

**Common Pythagorean triples** (sets of three integers that work in the Pythagorean theorem):
- 3, 4, 5
- 5, 12, 13
- 8, 15, 17
- 7, 24, 25

**Multiples also work:**
- 3, 4, 5 → 6, 8, 10 → 9, 12, 15, etc.

### 45-45-90 Triangles

**Angles:** 45°, 45°, 90°
**Sides:** If legs = x, then hypotenuse = x√2

**Example:**
```
Legs = 5
Hypotenuse = 5√2 ≈ 7.07
```

### 30-60-90 Triangles

**Angles:** 30°, 60°, 90°
**Sides:** If short leg = x, then:
- Long leg = x√3
- Hypotenuse = 2x

**Example:**
```
Short leg = 4
Long leg = 4√3 ≈ 6.93
Hypotenuse = 8
```

## Key Vocabulary

- **Volume:** The amount of space inside a 3D figure (cubic units)
- **Surface Area:** The total area of all faces of a 3D figure
- **Cross-Section:** A 2D shape formed by slicing a 3D figure
- **Prism:** A 3D figure with two parallel, congruent bases
- **Pyramid:** A 3D figure with one base and triangular faces meeting at an apex
- **Cylinder:** A 3D figure with two circular bases
- **Cone:** A 3D figure with one circular base and curved surface meeting at an apex
- **Sphere:** A 3D figure where all points are equidistant from the center
- **Complementary Angles:** Angles that sum to 90°
- **Supplementary Angles:** Angles that sum to 180°
- **Vertical Angles:** Opposite angles formed by intersecting lines (congruent)
- **Pythagorean Theorem:** a² + b² = c² for right triangles

## Practice Tips

1. Always label your units (cm³, in², etc.)
2. Volume is in cubic units, surface area in square units
3. For cylinders and cones, remember to find radius from diameter
4. Draw diagrams to visualize 3D figures
5. Triangle angles always sum to 180°
6. In Pythagorean theorem, c is always the hypotenuse (longest side)
7. Check if numbers are Pythagorean triples before calculating
8. Vertical angles are always equal
9. Use π ≈ 3.14 unless told otherwise
10. Remember: pyramids and cones use ⅓ in volume formulas
