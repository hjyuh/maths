## Lesson 1: Understanding Unit Rates

### What is a Unit Rate?

A **unit rate** is a rate with a denominator of 1. It tells us "how much per one."

**Examples of Unit Rates:**
- Miles per hour (mph)
- Cost per item
- Calories per serving
- Words per minute

### Finding Unit Rates

**Method:** Divide to find the amount per 1 unit

**Example 1:** Finding cost per item
```
6 apples cost $4.50
Unit rate = $4.50 ÷ 6 = $0.75 per apple
```

**Example 2:** Finding speed
```
A car travels 240 miles in 4 hours
Unit rate = 240 ÷ 4 = 60 miles per hour
```

**Example 3:** Finding rate of work
```
A machine produces 180 widgets in 6 hours
Unit rate = 180 ÷ 6 = 30 widgets per hour
```

### Unit Rate vs. Rate

**Rate:** A comparison of two quantities with different units
- Example: 120 miles in 3 hours

**Unit Rate:** A rate where the second quantity is 1
- Example: 40 miles per 1 hour (or 40 mph)

## Lesson 2: Using Unit Rates to Compare

### Comparing Using Unit Rates

When comparing options, find the unit rate for each and compare them.

**Example 4:** Which is the better buy?
```
Option A: 3 pounds for $7.50
Option B: 5 pounds for $11.50

Option A unit rate: $7.50 ÷ 3 = $2.50 per pound
Option B unit rate: $11.50 ÷ 5 = $2.30 per pound

Option B is the better buy!
```

**Example 5:** Who types faster?
```
Person A: 300 words in 5 minutes
Person B: 250 words in 4 minutes

Person A unit rate: 300 ÷ 5 = 60 words per minute
Person B unit rate: 250 ÷ 4 = 62.5 words per minute

Person B types faster!
```

## Lesson 3: Converting Rates

### Converting Unit Rates

Sometimes we need to convert between different units.

**Common Conversions:**
- 1 hour = 60 minutes
- 1 minute = 60 seconds
- 1 mile = 5,280 feet
- 1 foot = 12 inches
- 1 pound = 16 ounces
- 1 gallon = 4 quarts

**Example 6:** Convert miles per hour to feet per second
```
A car travels at 60 miles per hour.
How many feet per second is this?

Step 1: Convert miles to feet
60 miles × 5,280 feet/mile = 316,800 feet per hour

Step 2: Convert hours to seconds
1 hour = 3,600 seconds

Step 3: Find rate in feet per second
316,800 ÷ 3,600 = 88 feet per second
```

**Example 7:** Converting price per pound to price per ounce
```
Cheese costs $8 per pound.
What is the cost per ounce?

1 pound = 16 ounces
$8 ÷ 16 = $0.50 per ounce
```

## Lesson 4: Understanding Percent

### What is Percent?

**Percent** means "per hundred" or "out of 100."

The symbol for percent is **%**

**Key Concept:** Percent is a special ratio that compares a number to 100.

### Percent as a Fraction and Decimal

**Converting between forms:**

| Percent | Fraction | Decimal |
|---------|----------|---------|
| 25% | 25/100 = 1/4 | 0.25 |
| 50% | 50/100 = 1/2 | 0.50 |
| 75% | 75/100 = 3/4 | 0.75 |
| 100% | 100/100 = 1 | 1.00 |
| 10% | 10/100 = 1/10 | 0.10 |

### Converting Percent to Decimal

**Rule:** Divide by 100 (move decimal point 2 places left)

```
35% = 35 ÷ 100 = 0.35
8% = 8 ÷ 100 = 0.08
125% = 125 ÷ 100 = 1.25
```

### Converting Decimal to Percent

**Rule:** Multiply by 100 (move decimal point 2 places right)

```
0.42 = 0.42 × 100 = 42%
0.07 = 0.07 × 100 = 7%
1.5 = 1.5 × 100 = 150%
```

## Lesson 5: Finding Percent of a Number

### Method 1: Convert to Decimal and Multiply

**Example 8:** Find 30% of 80
```
30% = 0.30
0.30 × 80 = 24

30% of 80 is 24
```

**Example 9:** Find 15% of 60
```
15% = 0.15
0.15 × 60 = 9

15% of 60 is 9
```

### Method 2: Use Fractions

**Example 10:** Find 25% of 120
```
25% = 1/4
1/4 × 120 = 120 ÷ 4 = 30

25% of 120 is 30
```

### Method 3: Find 10% First (Benchmark Strategy)

**Example 11:** Find 30% of 150
```
Step 1: Find 10% of 150
10% of 150 = 15

Step 2: Multiply by 3 (for 30%)
30% = 3 × 15 = 45

30% of 150 is 45
```

## Lesson 6: Percent Problems - Finding the Part

### The Percent Equation

**Part = Percent × Whole**

**Example 12:** What is 40% of 90?
```
Part = 0.40 × 90
Part = 36

40% of 90 is 36
```

**Example 13:** A store has 200 items. 35% are on sale. How many items are on sale?
```
Part = 0.35 × 200
Part = 70

70 items are on sale
```

---

## Lesson 7: Percent Problems - Finding the Whole

### When the Part and Percent are Known

**Formula:** Whole = Part ÷ Percent (as decimal)

**Example 14:** 20% of what number is 15?
```
15 = 0.20 × Whole
Whole = 15 ÷ 0.20
Whole = 75

20% of 75 is 15
```

**Example 15:** A student answered 18 questions correctly, which was 75% of the test. How many questions were on the test?
```
18 = 0.75 × Total questions
Total = 18 ÷ 0.75
Total = 24 questions
```

---

## Lesson 8: Percent Problems - Finding the Percent

### When the Part and Whole are Known

**Formula:** Percent = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

**Example 16:** What percent of 80 is 20?
```
Percent = (20 ÷ 80) × 100
Percent = 0.25 × 100
Percent = 25%

20 is 25% of 80
```

**Example 17:** A basketball player made 15 out of 25 free throws. What percent did she make?
```
Percent = (15 ÷ 25) × 100
Percent = 0.60 × 100
Percent = 60%

She made 60% of her free throws
```

## Lesson 9: Percent Increase and Decrease

### Finding Percent Change

**Percent Change = (Amount of Change ÷ Original Amount) × 100**

### Percent Increase

**Example 18:** A shirt's price increased from $20 to $25. What is the percent increase?
```
Change = $25 - $20 = $5
Percent increase = ($5 ÷ $20) × 100
Percent increase = 0.25 × 100 = 25%

The price increased by 25%
```

### Percent Decrease

**Example 19:** A population decreased from 500 to 400. What is the percent decrease?
```
Change = 500 - 400 = 100
Percent decrease = (100 ÷ 500) × 100
Percent decrease = 0.20 × 100 = 20%

The population decreased by 20%
```

## Lesson 10: Real-World Applications

### Sales Tax

**Example 20:** An item costs $50. Sales tax is 6%. What is the total cost?
```
Tax = 6% of $50
Tax = 0.06 × $50 = $3

Total cost = $50 + $3 = $53
```

### Discounts

**Example 21:** A $80 jacket is on sale for 25% off. What is the sale price?
```
Discount = 25% of $80
Discount = 0.25 × $80 = $20

Sale price = $80 - $20 = $60
```

### Tips

**Example 22:** A restaurant bill is $45. You want to leave a 20% tip. How much is the tip?
```
Tip = 20% of $45
Tip = 0.20 × $45 = $9

Total = $45 + $9 = $54
```

### Interest (Simple)

**Example 23:** You deposit $200 in a savings account that earns 5% interest per year. How much interest do you earn in one year?
```
Interest = 5% of $200
Interest = 0.05 × $200 = $10

You earn $10 in interest
```

## Key Vocabulary

- **Unit Rate:** A rate with a denominator of 1
- **Rate:** A ratio that compares two quantities with different units
- **Percent:** A ratio that compares a number to 100
- **Percent of a Number:** A part of a whole expressed as a percent
- **Percent Increase:** The percent by which a quantity grows
- **Percent Decrease:** The percent by which a quantity shrinks
- **Sales Tax:** A percent of a purchase price added as tax
- **Discount:** A percent reduction in price
- **Tip:** A percent of a bill given for service

## Practice Tips

1. **For unit rates:** Always divide to get "per one"
2. **When comparing:** Calculate unit rates for all options before deciding
3. **Converting percent to decimal:** Move decimal 2 places left
4. **Converting decimal to percent:** Move decimal 2 places right
5. **Finding percent of a number:** Convert percent to decimal, then multiply
6. **Remember the formula triangle:**
   ```
        Part
       ------
   Percent  Whole
   ```
7. **Check reasonableness:** Does your answer make sense in context?
8. **For percent change:** Always divide by the ORIGINAL amount

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Forgetting to convert percent to decimal**
   - Incorrect: 20% of 50 = 20 × 50 = 1000
   - Correct: 20% of 50 = 0.20 × 50 = 10

2. **Using the wrong value for percent change**
   - Always divide by the ORIGINAL value, not the new value

3. **Confusing percent with the actual number**
   - 50% is not 50, it's 0.50 or 1/2

4. **Rounding too early**
   - Keep extra decimal places until the final answer

5. **Not including units in the answer**
   - Always include units (dollars, miles per hour, etc.)

6. **Mixing up increase and decrease**
   - Increase: Add the change
   - Decrease: Subtract the change
