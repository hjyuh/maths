## Part 1: Theoretical Probability (20 points)

**1.** (4 points) You roll a standard die. What is P(rolling a number greater than 4)?

**2.** (4 points) A bag contains 5 red, 3 blue, and 7 green marbles. What is P(drawing a blue marble)?

**3.** (6 points) You draw a card from a standard deck. What is P(drawing a king or a queen)?

**4.** (6 points) You roll a die. What is P(NOT rolling a 3)?

---

## Part 2: Experimental Probability (15 points)

**5.** (5 points) A basketball player made 18 out of 24 free throw attempts. What is the experimental probability of making a free throw? Express as a fraction in simplest form.

**6.** (5 points) A spinner was spun 80 times and landed on red 20 times. What is the experimental P(red)?

**7.** (5 points) In 100 trials of an experiment, an event occurred 35 times. Based on this, if you run 200 more trials, how many times would you expect the event to occur?

---

## Part 3: Compound Events - Sample Space (15 points)

**8.** (5 points) You flip a coin and roll a die. List the complete sample space.

**9.** (5 points) Two coins are flipped. What is P(getting at least one heads)?

**10.** (5 points) Two dice are rolled. What is P(the sum equals 9)?

---

## Part 4: Independent Events (20 points)

**11.** (6 points) You flip a coin twice. What is P(heads on first flip AND tails on second flip)?

**12.** (7 points) You roll two dice. What is P(rolling a 4 on the first die AND rolling an even number on the second die)?

**13.** (7 points) You draw a card from a deck, replace it, then draw again. What is P(both cards are hearts)?

---

## Part 5: Dependent Events (15 points)

**14.** (8 points) A bag contains 6 red marbles and 4 blue marbles. You draw two marbles without replacement. What is P(first marble is red AND second marble is blue)?

**15.** (7 points) You draw two cards from a standard deck without replacement. What is P(both cards are aces)?

---

## Part 6: Real-World Application (15 points)

**16.** (7 points) A weather forecast says there is a 40% chance of rain tomorrow. What is the probability it will NOT rain?

**17.** (8 points) A quality control inspector finds that 3% of items produced are defective. If the factory produces 5,000 items in a day, how many would you expect to be defective?

---

## Bonus Question (5 points)

**18.** A game involves rolling two dice. You win if the sum is 7 or 11.
   a) What is P(sum is 7)?
   b) What is P(sum is 11)?
   c) What is P(winning the game)?

## Answer Key (For Teacher Use)

**Part 1: Theoretical Probability**
1. 1/3 (outcomes 5 and 6 out of 6 total)
2. 1/5 (3 blue out of 15 total)
3. 2/13 (8 cards out of 52)
4. 5/6

**Part 2: Experimental Probability**
5. 3/4 or 18/24
6. 1/4 or 20/80
7. 70 times (35% of 200)

**Part 3: Compound Events**
8. {H1, H2, H3, H4, H5, H6, T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, T6} (12 outcomes)
9. 3/4 (outcomes HH, HT, TH out of 4 total)
10. 1/9 (4 outcomes: (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3) out of 36)

**Part 4: Independent Events**
11. 1/4
12. 1/12 (P(4) × P(even) = 1/6 × 1/2 = 1/12)
13. 1/16 (1/4 × 1/4)

**Part 5: Dependent Events**
14. 4/15 (6/10 × 4/9 = 24/90 = 4/15)
15. 1/221 (4/52 × 3/51 = 12/2652 = 1/221)

**Part 6: Real-World Application**
16. 60% or 0.6
17. 150 items (0.03 × 5000 = 150)

**Bonus**
18. a) P(sum = 7) = 6/36 = 1/6
    b) P(sum = 11) = 2/36 = 1/18
    c) P(winning) = 1/6 + 1/18 = 3/18 + 1/18 = 4/18 = 2/9
