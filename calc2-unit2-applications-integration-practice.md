## Section 1: Work - Springs and Hooke's Law

1. A spring has a natural length of 20 cm. A force of 30 N stretches it to 24 cm. How much work is done stretching it from 22 cm to 28 cm?

2. A spring requires 10 J of work to stretch it 0.1 m from its natural length. Find the spring constant k.

3. Two identical springs, each with spring constant k = 200 N/m, are attached end to end. How much work is required to stretch this system 0.4 m from its natural length?

4. A spring has spring constant 150 N/m. Find the work required to stretch it from 0.1 m to 0.2 m beyond its natural length.

5. A force of 40 N compresses a spring 0.05 m. How much work is done compressing it from 0.02 m to 0.08 m?

---

## Section 2: Work - Ropes and Cables

6. A 60-ft chain weighing 2 lb/ft hangs from a windlass. How much work is done winding up the entire chain?

7. A rope 100 feet long and weighing 0.8 lb/ft hangs over the edge of a building 100 feet tall. How much work is required to pull the rope to the top?

8. A 50-meter cable weighing 3 N/m hangs from a crane. How much work is done winding up 30 meters of the cable?

9. A chain 20 m long with mass 30 kg hangs vertically. How much work is required to raise the top half of the chain to the top?

10. A rope of length L and weight w per unit length hangs from a building of height h > L. Find the work required to pull the rope to the top.

## Section 3: Work - Pumping Liquids

11. A cylindrical tank with radius 2 m and height 6 m is full of water. How much work is required to pump all the water to the top of the tank? (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)

12. A conical tank (vertex down) has radius 3 m and height 5 m. It is filled with water to a depth of 4 m. Find the work required to pump all the water out the top.

13. A hemispherical tank of radius 4 m is full of water. Find the work required to pump all the water to a point 2 m above the top of the tank.

14. A rectangular tank 4 ft wide, 6 ft long, and 5 ft deep is full of water (62.4 lb/ft³). How much work is required to pump the water to a height 3 ft above the top of the tank?

15. A spherical tank of radius 3 m is half full of oil (density 900 kg/m³). Find the work required to pump all the oil out the top.

## Section 4: Fluid Pressure - Rectangular Surfaces

16. A rectangular plate 3 m wide and 4 m tall is submerged vertically in water with its top edge 1 m below the surface. Find the fluid force on one side.

17. A vertical rectangular gate 6 ft wide and 4 ft high is in a dam. The water level is at the top of the gate. Find the total force on the gate. (ρ = 62.4 lb/ft³)

18. A rectangular swimming pool viewing window is 2 m wide and 1.5 m tall, with its top edge 2 m below the water surface. Find the force on the window.

19. An aquarium side wall is 8 ft long and 3 ft high. Find the force on this wall when the tank is full.

20. A rectangular floodgate is 10 m wide and 5 m tall, submerged vertically with its top edge at the water surface. Find the total force.

## Section 6: Center of Mass - Discrete Points
