## Lesson 1: Understanding Percents

### What is a Percent?

**Percent** means "per hundred" or "out of 100"

**Key concept:** Percent is a ratio that compares a number to 100

**Examples:**
- 25% = 25/100 = 0.25
- 50% = 50/100 = 0.50 = 1/2
- 75% = 75/100 = 0.75 = 3/4
- 100% = 100/100 = 1 (the whole)

### Converting Between Forms

**Percent to Decimal:** Divide by 100 (move decimal 2 places left)
```
35% = 0.35
8% = 0.08
125% = 1.25
```

**Decimal to Percent:** Multiply by 100 (move decimal 2 places right)
```
0.45 = 45%
0.06 = 6%
1.5 = 150%
```

**Percent to Fraction:** Write over 100 and simplify
```
40% = 40/100 = 2/5
75% = 75/100 = 3/4
```

**Fraction to Percent:** Divide, then multiply by 100
```
3/5 = 0.6 = 60%
1/4 = 0.25 = 25%
```

## Lesson 2: Finding the Percent of a Number

### Method 1: Multiply by Decimal

**Steps:**
1. Convert percent to decimal
2. Multiply by the number

**Example 1:** Find 30% of 80
```
30% = 0.30
0.30 × 80 = 24
```

**Example 2:** Find 15% of 60
```
15% = 0.15
0.15 × 60 = 9
```

### Method 2: Use Proportions

**Set up:** part/whole = percent/100

**Example:** Find 25% of 48
```
x/48 = 25/100
100x = 1200
x = 12
```

## Lesson 3: Finding What Percent One Number is of Another

### The Question: "What percent of A is B?"

**Formula:** (part ÷ whole) × 100 = percent

**Example 1:** What percent of 50 is 15?
```
15 ÷ 50 = 0.30
0.30 × 100 = 30%
```

**Example 2:** 24 is what percent of 80?
```
24 ÷ 80 = 0.30
0.30 × 100 = 30%
```

**Example 3:** What percent of 25 is 30?
```
30 ÷ 25 = 1.20
1.20 × 100 = 120%
(More than 100% because 30 > 25)
```

## Lesson 4: Finding the Whole When Given a Percent

### The Question: "A is B% of what number?"

**Method:** Divide the part by the decimal form of the percent

**Example 1:** 18 is 30% of what number?
```
18 ÷ 0.30 = 60
```

**Example 2:** 15 is 25% of what number?
```
15 ÷ 0.25 = 60
```

**Example 3:** 24 is 120% of what number?
```
24 ÷ 1.20 = 20
```

## Lesson 5: Percent Increase and Decrease

### Percent Change Formula

**Percent Change = (Amount of Change ÷ Original Amount) × 100**

### Percent Increase

**Example 1:** A price increases from $40 to $50
```
Change: 50 - 40 = 10
Percent increase: (10 ÷ 40) × 100 = 25%
```

**Example 2:** Population grows from 200 to 250
```
Change: 250 - 200 = 50
Percent increase: (50 ÷ 200) × 100 = 25%
```

### Percent Decrease

**Example 1:** A price drops from $80 to $60
```
Change: 80 - 60 = 20
Percent decrease: (20 ÷ 80) × 100 = 25%
```

**Example 2:** Temperature falls from 20°C to 15°C
```
Change: 20 - 15 = 5
Percent decrease: (5 ÷ 20) × 100 = 25%
```

**Important:** Always use the **original** amount as the denominator!

## Lesson 6: Finding New Amounts After Percent Change

### After a Percent Increase

**Method 1:** Find the increase, then add
```
Example: Increase $50 by 20%
Increase: 0.20 × 50 = 10
New amount: 50 + 10 = $60
```

**Method 2:** Multiply by (1 + percent as decimal)
```
Example: Increase $50 by 20%
New amount: 50 × 1.20 = $60
```

### After a Percent Decrease

**Method 1:** Find the decrease, then subtract
```
Example: Decrease $80 by 25%
Decrease: 0.25 × 80 = 20
New amount: 80 - 20 = $60
```

**Method 2:** Multiply by (1 - percent as decimal)
```
Example: Decrease $80 by 25%
New amount: 80 × 0.75 = $60
```

## Lesson 7: Applications of Percents

### Sales Tax

**Example:** An item costs $45. Sales tax is 8%. Find the total.
```
Tax: 0.08 × 45 = $3.60
Total: 45 + 3.60 = $48.60

Or: 45 × 1.08 = $48.60
```

### Tips and Gratuity

**Example:** A meal costs $32. You want to leave a 15% tip.
```
Tip: 0.15 × 32 = $4.80
Total: 32 + 4.80 = $36.80
```

### Discounts

**Example:** A $60 shirt is 30% off. Find the sale price.
```
Discount: 0.30 × 60 = $18
Sale price: 60 - 18 = $42

Or: 60 × 0.70 = $42
```

### Markup

**Example:** A store buys items for $20 and marks them up 40%.
```
Markup: 0.40 × 20 = $8
Selling price: 20 + 8 = $28

Or: 20 × 1.40 = $28
```

## Lesson 8: Introduction to Statistics

### Population vs. Sample

**Population:** The entire group you want information about
**Sample:** A subset of the population used to gather information

**Example:**
- Population: All students in a school (1000 students)
- Sample: 100 randomly selected students from that school

### Why Use Samples?

1. Populations can be too large to survey
2. Saves time and money
3. Can still provide accurate information if done properly

### Random Sampling

**Random sample:** Every member of the population has an equal chance of being selected

**Good random sample methods:**
- Drawing names from a hat
- Using a random number generator
- Systematic selection (every 10th person)

**Biased samples to avoid:**
- Only surveying friends
- Only surveying people in one location
- Self-selected surveys (only people who want to respond)

## Lesson 9: Using Samples to Make Predictions

### Making Inferences About Populations

**Steps:**
1. Collect data from random sample
2. Calculate the percent or proportion in the sample
3. Apply that percent to the entire population

**Example 1:** Predicting preferences
```
Sample: 40 out of 100 students prefer pizza
Percent: 40/100 = 40%
Population: 500 students total
Prediction: 0.40 × 500 = 200 students prefer pizza
```

**Example 2:** Quality control
```
Sample: 3 defective items out of 150 tested
Percent: 3/150 = 2%
Production: 10,000 items made
Prediction: 0.02 × 10,000 = 200 defective items
```

## Lesson 10: Measures of Center

### Mean (Average)

**Definition:** The sum of all values divided by the number of values

**Formula:** Mean = (Sum of values) ÷ (Number of values)

**Example:** Find the mean of 5, 8, 12, 15, 10
```
Sum: 5 + 8 + 12 + 15 + 10 = 50
Number of values: 5
Mean: 50 ÷ 5 = 10
```

### Median

**Definition:** The middle value when data is arranged in order

**Steps:**
1. Arrange data in order
2. Find the middle value
3. If two middle values, find their average

**Example 1:** Find the median of 3, 7, 5, 9, 4
```
In order: 3, 4, 5, 7, 9
Middle value: 5
Median = 5
```

**Example 2:** Find the median of 2, 5, 8, 10
```
In order: 2, 5, 8, 10
Two middle values: 5 and 8
Median = (5 + 8) ÷ 2 = 6.5
```

### Mode

**Definition:** The value that appears most frequently

**Example 1:** Find the mode of 2, 5, 3, 5, 7, 5, 9
```
5 appears most frequently (3 times)
Mode = 5
```

**Example 2:** Find the mode of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
```
No value repeats
No mode
```

## Lesson 11: Measures of Variability

### Range

**Definition:** The difference between the maximum and minimum values

**Formula:** Range = Maximum - Minimum

**Example:** Find the range of 3, 7, 12, 5, 18
```
Maximum: 18
Minimum: 3
Range: 18 - 3 = 15
```

### Interquartile Range (IQR)

**Definition:** The range of the middle 50% of data

**Quartiles:**
- Q1 (first quartile): The median of the lower half
- Q2 (second quartile): The median of all data
- Q3 (third quartile): The median of the upper half

**IQR = Q3 - Q1**

**Example:** Find IQR of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14
```
Q2 (median): 8
Lower half: 2, 4, 6 → Q1 = 4
Upper half: 10, 12, 14 → Q3 = 12
IQR = 12 - 4 = 8
```

### Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD)

**Definition:** The average distance of each data point from the mean

**Steps:**
1. Find the mean
2. Find the distance of each value from the mean
3. Find the average of those distances

**Example:** Find MAD of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10
```
Mean: 6
Distances from mean: |2-6|=4, |4-6|=2, |6-6|=0, |8-6|=2, |10-6|=4
Average of distances: (4+2+0+2+4)/5 = 12/5 = 2.4
MAD = 2.4
```

## Lesson 12: Comparing Data Sets

### Using Measures of Center and Variability

**To compare data sets, examine:**
1. **Center:** Which has a higher mean or median?
2. **Variability:** Which has more spread (range, IQR, MAD)?

**Example:** Compare test scores
```
Class A: Mean = 85, Range = 20
Class B: Mean = 85, Range = 10

Analysis:
- Both classes have same average performance
- Class B scores are more consistent (less variability)
- Class A has more varied performance levels
```

## Key Vocabulary

- **Percent:** A ratio that compares a number to 100
- **Percent Increase/Decrease:** The percent change from an original amount
- **Discount:** A reduction in price (percent decrease)
- **Markup:** An increase in price (percent increase)
- **Population:** The entire group being studied
- **Sample:** A subset of the population
- **Random Sample:** A sample where every member has equal chance of selection
- **Bias:** A systematic error that makes results unrepresentative
- **Mean:** The average of a data set
- **Median:** The middle value when data is ordered
- **Mode:** The most frequent value
- **Range:** The difference between max and min values
- **Interquartile Range (IQR):** The range of the middle 50% of data

---

## Practice Tips

1. Convert percents to decimals by dividing by 100
2. Always use the original amount for percent change calculations
3. Check if your answer makes sense (e.g., 150% is more than the whole)
4. For percent increase: multiply by (1 + percent)
5. For percent decrease: multiply by (1 - percent)
6. Random samples should be unbiased
7. Larger samples generally give better predictions
8. Mean is affected by outliers; median is not
9. Always arrange data in order before finding median
10. Check your calculations with estimation
