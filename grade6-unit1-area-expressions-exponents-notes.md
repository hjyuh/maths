## Lesson 1: Area of Polygons

### Finding Area by Decomposition

**Key Concept:** Complex polygons can be broken down into simpler shapes (rectangles and triangles) to find their total area.

**Area Formulas:**
- Rectangle: A = length × width
- Triangle: A = ½ × base × height
- Parallelogram: A = base × height
- Trapezoid: A = ½ × (base₁ + base₂) × height

**Strategy: Decomposing Polygons**
1. Identify familiar shapes within the polygon
2. Calculate the area of each component
3. Add all areas together

**Example 1:** Find the area of an L-shaped figure
```
If an L-shape can be divided into two rectangles:
- Rectangle 1: 5 cm × 3 cm = 15 cm²
- Rectangle 2: 4 cm × 2 cm = 8 cm²
- Total Area = 15 + 8 = 23 cm²
```

**Example 2:** Find the area of a triangle
```
Base = 10 cm, Height = 6 cm
Area = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm²
```

## Lesson 2: Surface Area and Nets

### What is a Net?

A **net** is a two-dimensional pattern that can be folded to form a three-dimensional figure.

**Key Concept:** Surface area is the total area of all faces of a 3D figure.

### Surface Area of Rectangular Prisms

**Formula:** SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)

Where:
- l = length
- w = width
- h = height

**Example:**
```
For a box with l = 5 cm, w = 3 cm, h = 4 cm:
SA = 2(5×3 + 5×4 + 3×4)
SA = 2(15 + 20 + 12)
SA = 2(47)
SA = 94 cm²
```

### Surface Area Using Nets

**Method:**
1. Draw or visualize the net of the 3D figure
2. Identify all faces (rectangles and triangles)
3. Calculate the area of each face
4. Add all areas together

## Lesson 3: Writing Algebraic Expressions

### Parts of an Expression

**Term:** A single number or variable, or numbers and variables multiplied together
- Examples: 5, x, 3y, 7ab

**Coefficient:** The numerical factor of a term
- In 3x, the coefficient is 3
- In -5y, the coefficient is -5

**Variable:** A letter that represents an unknown value

**Constant:** A term without a variable
- Examples: 5, -2, 10

### Translating Words to Expressions

| Words | Expression |
|-------|------------|
| The sum of x and 5 | x + 5 |
| 7 less than y | y - 7 |
| The product of 3 and n | 3n or 3 × n |
| x divided by 4 | x/4 or x ÷ 4 |
| 5 more than twice a number | 2n + 5 |

**Example:** Write an expression for "subtract y from 5"
```
Answer: 5 - y
(Note: Order matters in subtraction!)
```

## Lesson 4: Exponents

### Understanding Exponents

**Exponent notation:** base^exponent

- 2³ means 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
- 5² means 5 × 5 = 25
- 10⁴ means 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 10,000

**Key Terms:**
- **Base:** The number being multiplied
- **Exponent:** How many times to multiply the base by itself
- **Power:** The entire expression (e.g., "2 to the third power")

### Evaluating Expressions with Exponents

**Order of Operations (PEMDAS):**
1. **P**arentheses
2. **E**xponents
3. **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision (left to right)
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right)

**Example 1:** Evaluate 3² + 4
```
= 9 + 4
= 13
```

**Example 2:** Evaluate 2(3³ - 7)
```
= 2(27 - 7)
= 2(20)
= 40
```

## Lesson 5: Evaluating Expressions

### Substitution

To evaluate an expression when variables have specific values:
1. Replace each variable with its given value
2. Follow the order of operations

**Example:** Evaluate 3x + 5 when x = 4
```
= 3(4) + 5
= 12 + 5
= 17
```

**Example:** Evaluate 2a² - b when a = 3 and b = 5
```
= 2(3)² - 5
= 2(9) - 5
= 18 - 5
= 13
```

## Lesson 6: GCF and LCM

### Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The **GCF** is the largest number that divides evenly into two or more numbers.

**Method 1: List factors**
```
Find GCF of 12 and 18:
Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6
GCF = 6
```

**Method 2: Prime factorization**
```
12 = 2² × 3
18 = 2 × 3²
GCF = 2 × 3 = 6
```

### Least Common Multiple (LCM)

The **LCM** is the smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

**Method: List multiples**
```
Find LCM of 4 and 6:
Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24...
Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30...
Common multiples: 12, 24...
LCM = 12
```

### Using the Distributive Property with GCF

**Example:** Express 18 + 48 as a product using the GCF
```
GCF of 18 and 48 = 6
18 + 48 = 6(3 + 8)
```

## Lesson 7: Variables in Real-World Problems

### Writing Expressions for Situations

**Example 1:** Cost of apples
```
If apples cost $2 per pound, and you buy p pounds:
Total cost = 2p
```

**Example 2:** Perimeter of a rectangle
```
If length is l and width is w:
Perimeter = 2l + 2w or 2(l + w)
```

**Example 3:** Age problems
```
If Maria is m years old and her brother is 3 years younger:
Brother's age = m - 3
```

## Key Vocabulary

- **Area:** The amount of space inside a 2D figure (square units)
- **Surface Area:** The total area of all faces of a 3D figure
- **Net:** A 2D pattern that folds into a 3D figure
- **Expression:** A mathematical phrase with numbers, variables, and operations
- **Variable:** A letter representing an unknown value
- **Exponent:** The number of times a base is multiplied by itself
- **GCF:** Greatest Common Factor
- **LCM:** Least Common Multiple
- **Coefficient:** The number multiplied by a variable
- **Term:** A part of an expression separated by + or - signs

## Practice Tips

1. When finding area, always include square units (cm², in², etc.)
2. Draw nets to visualize 3D figures
3. Follow PEMDAS for order of operations
4. Check that your expression makes sense in context
5. Show all steps when evaluating expressions
