## Lesson 1: Understanding Ratios

### What is a Ratio?

A **ratio** is a comparison of two quantities by division. Ratios can compare:
- Part to part (boys to girls)
- Part to whole (boys to total students)
- Whole to part (total students to girls)

**Key Concept:** Ratios show the relationship between two or more quantities.

### Three Ways to Write Ratios

If there are 3 cats and 5 dogs:

1. **With the word "to":** 3 to 5
2. **With a colon:** 3:5
3. **As a fraction:** 3/5

**Important:** All three forms mean the same thing, but the order matters!
- The ratio of cats to dogs is 3:5
- The ratio of dogs to cats is 5:3

### Example 1: Writing Ratios
```
A classroom has 12 boys and 15 girls.

Ratio of boys to girls: 12 to 15 or 12:15 or 12/15
Ratio of girls to boys: 15 to 12 or 15:12 or 15/12
Ratio of boys to total: 12 to 27 or 12:27 or 12/27
```

### Example 2: Ratios from Real Life
```
A recipe uses 2 cups of flour and 3 cups of water.
Ratio of flour to water: 2:3
This means for every 2 cups of flour, we need 3 cups of water.
```

## Lesson 2: Equivalent Ratios

### What are Equivalent Ratios?

**Equivalent ratios** express the same relationship between quantities.

Just like equivalent fractions, you can multiply or divide both parts of a ratio by the same number to create an equivalent ratio.

### Creating Equivalent Ratios

**Method: Multiply both parts by the same number**

```
Starting ratio: 2:3

Multiply by 2: (2×2):(3×2) = 4:6
Multiply by 3: (2×3):(3×3) = 6:9
Multiply by 4: (2×4):(3×4) = 8:12

All ratios 2:3, 4:6, 6:9, and 8:12 are equivalent!
```

### Simplifying Ratios

**Method: Divide both parts by their GCF**

```
Simplify 12:18

GCF of 12 and 18 = 6
12÷6 : 18÷6 = 2:3

The simplified ratio is 2:3
```

### Example 3: Are These Ratios Equivalent?
```
Are 3:4 and 15:20 equivalent?

Method 1: Simplify 15:20
GCF of 15 and 20 = 5
15÷5 : 20÷5 = 3:4 ✓

Method 2: Cross multiply (when written as fractions)
3/4 and 15/20
3 × 20 = 60
4 × 15 = 60
Since 60 = 60, they are equivalent! ✓
```

## Lesson 3: Using Ratio Tables

### What is a Ratio Table?

A **ratio table** shows several equivalent ratios in an organized way.

### Creating a Ratio Table

**Example:** A recipe uses 2 eggs for every 3 cups of flour.

| Eggs | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|------|---|---|---|---|-----|
| Flour | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 |

Notice: Each ratio column (2:3, 4:6, 6:9, etc.) is equivalent!

### Using Ratio Tables to Solve Problems

**Example:** If you use 12 eggs, how many cups of flour do you need?

| Eggs | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|------|---|---|---|---|-----|-----|
| Flour | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 | 18 |

Answer: You need 18 cups of flour.

**Strategy:** We multiplied the original ratio by 6: (2×6):(3×6) = 12:18

## Lesson 4: Part-to-Part and Part-to-Whole Ratios

### Understanding Different Types of Ratios

**Part-to-Part Ratio:** Compares one part to another part
**Part-to-Whole Ratio:** Compares one part to the total

### Example 4: Paint Mixture
```
A paint mixture uses 3 parts blue and 2 parts yellow.

Part-to-Part Ratios:
- Blue to yellow: 3:2
- Yellow to blue: 2:3

Part-to-Whole Ratios:
- Total parts = 3 + 2 = 5
- Blue to total: 3:5
- Yellow to total: 2:5
```

### Example 5: Classroom Problem
```
In a class of 30 students, the ratio of boys to girls is 3:2.

Step 1: Find total parts: 3 + 2 = 5 parts
Step 2: Find value of one part: 30 ÷ 5 = 6 students
Step 3: Find each quantity:
- Boys: 3 × 6 = 18 students
- Girls: 2 × 6 = 12 students

Check: 18 + 12 = 30 ✓
```

## Lesson 5: Using Diagrams with Ratios

### Tape Diagrams

Tape diagrams (also called bar models) help visualize ratios.

**Example:** The ratio of savings to spending is 4:1

```

Double number lines show equivalent ratios on parallel lines.

**Example:** 3 pounds of apples cost $6

```

         |    |    |    |    |

## Lesson 6: Real-World Ratio Problems

### Strategy for Solving Ratio Problems

1. **Identify** what quantities are being compared
2. **Write** the ratio in the correct order
3. **Create** equivalent ratios or a ratio table
4. **Solve** using the table or by scaling up/down
5. **Check** that your answer makes sense

### Example 6: Recipe Problem
```
A fruit punch recipe uses 4 cups of pineapple juice to 6 cups of orange juice.
If you use 20 cups of pineapple juice, how much orange juice do you need?

Ratio: 4:6
Simplified: 2:3

| Pineapple | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 20 |
|-----------|---|---|---|---|-----|-----|
| Orange    | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12| 15 | 30 |

Answer: 30 cups of orange juice
```

### Example 7: Map Scale
```
On a map, 2 inches represents 50 miles.
How many miles do 7 inches represent?

Set up ratio table:

| Inches | 2 | 4 | 6 | 7 |
|--------|---|---|---|-----|
| Miles  | 50| 100| 150| ? |

Method 1: Find rate per inch
50 ÷ 2 = 25 miles per inch
7 × 25 = 175 miles

Method 2: Scale the ratio
2:50 = 7:?
? = (7 × 50) ÷ 2 = 175 miles
```

## Lesson 7: Comparing Ratios

### When Two Ratios Are Given

Sometimes we need to compare two different ratios.

**Example:** Which is a better deal?
- Store A: 3 pounds of apples for $6
- Store B: 5 pounds of apples for $9

**Method 1: Find unit rates (cost per pound)**
- Store A: $6 ÷ 3 = $2 per pound
- Store B: $9 ÷ 5 = $1.80 per pound

Store B is the better deal!

**Method 2: Find common quantity**
Find the cost for 15 pounds (LCM of 3 and 5):
- Store A: 3:6 = 15:30 → $30 for 15 pounds
- Store B: 5:9 = 15:27 → $27 for 15 pounds

Store B is cheaper!

## Key Vocabulary

- **Ratio:** A comparison of two quantities by division
- **Equivalent Ratios:** Ratios that express the same relationship
- **Ratio Table:** An organized way to show equivalent ratios
- **Part-to-Part Ratio:** Compares one part to another part
- **Part-to-Whole Ratio:** Compares a part to the total
- **Tape Diagram:** A visual model using bars to represent ratios
- **Double Number Line:** Two parallel number lines showing equivalent ratios
- **Simplify:** To reduce a ratio to its smallest whole numbers using the GCF

---

## Practice Tips

1. **Always write ratios in the correct order** - "boys to girls" is different from "girls to boys"
2. **Use the GCF to simplify ratios** - just like simplifying fractions
3. **Create ratio tables** to organize your work and find equivalent ratios
4. **Draw diagrams** (tape diagrams or double number lines) to visualize problems
5. **Check your work** by verifying that your ratios are equivalent
6. **Pay attention to units** - are you comparing the same types of quantities?
7. **Look for keywords:**
   - "for every" indicates a ratio
   - "per" often indicates a unit rate
   - "to" compares two quantities

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Writing ratios in the wrong order**
   - Incorrect: If there are 3 cats and 5 dogs, the ratio of cats to dogs is 5:3
   - Correct: The ratio of cats to dogs is 3:5

2. **Forgetting to simplify**
   - 12:18 should be simplified to 2:3

3. **Adding or subtracting to find equivalent ratios**
   - Incorrect: 2:3 = 4:5 (added 2 to each)
   - Correct: 2:3 = 4:6 (multiplied both by 2)

4. **Confusing part-to-part with part-to-whole**
   - If the ratio of boys to girls is 3:2, this doesn't mean 3/2 of the class is boys
   - Boys are 3/5 of the class (part-to-whole)

5. **Not checking if answer makes sense**
   - Always verify your final answer in context
